0. Where this board sits in the NOVAZ80 project

The Z80 single-board computer: the CPU board for the "Z80 SBC" configuration. With nothing else stacked on it, it is a complete minimal computer (CPU, 32 K ROM, 32 K RAM, PIO, CTC). The optional I/O and VDP/sound boards add peripherals through the stack bus. The programmer card (V102-PR1-2) burns its ROM in circuit.

This board is also the power source for the stack. Its DC-DC module feeds +5 V onto SPEC pin 2.

Status: built, tested and in use. Section 9 collects observations for the planned 4-layer revision.

4. Subsystems

4.1 CPU (IC1)

  • Address and data buses go straight to the memories, PIO, CTC and the stack connectors. There are no bus buffers. The CPU drives every board in the stack directly.
  • Pulled up only, not on the bus: /NMI (R10) and /WAIT (R8). No other board can raise an NMI or insert wait states.
  • Not connected: /HALT, /RFSH, /BUSACK. This confirms the programmer's note: no BUSAK on the bus.
  • /BUSRQ: 1 k pull-up (R12), on PROG pin 3 for the programmer.
  • /INT: wired-OR of PIO and CTC (open-drain outputs), 1 k pull-up (R6), and on CTRL pin 8 so other boards can interrupt.
  • D0–D7 have 4k7 pull-ups, so an undriven bus reads FF. In IM 0 a floating-bus interrupt acknowledge reads as RST 38h.

4.2 Memory (IC2 ROM, IC3 RAM)

  • Both chips take A0–A14 from the CPU directly. A15 goes only to the PLD, so the PLD makes the 32 K / 32 K split.
  • ROM 28C256: /CS = CSB0 from the PLD. /OE = ROMOEX (PROG pin 7) with a 1 k pull-down, so the output is always enabled unless the programmer pulls it high. /WE = ROMWEX (PROG pin 4) with a 1 k pull-up, so the Z80 cannot write the EEPROM. Only the programmer can.
  • RAM 62256: /CS = CSB1. /OE is tied to GND. /WE = the CPU's /WR.
  • Because both /OE pins are permanently active, a chip drives the data bus as soon as its /CS is low, whether the cycle is a read or a write. See section 9, item 1.

4.3 PIO (IC4)

  • B/A = A0, C/D = A1. Ports: 20h port A data, 21h port B data, 22h port A control, 23h port B control.
  • /CE = CSB2 from the PLD. /M1, /IORQ, /RD, CLK and /INT come from the CPU bus.
  • /ASTB and /BSTB have 1 k pull-ups (R16, R7), so an unused handshake input sits inactive.
  • The Z80 PIO has no reset pin. It resets itself when it sees /M1 without /RD or /IORQ (which happens during CPU reset).
  • Ports and handshakes go to J1 (section 6.2).

4.4 CTC (IC5)

  • CS0 = A0, CS1 = A1, so channels 0–3 are at ports 18h–1Bh. /CE = CSB3.
  • /RESET = RSTB (the PLD's reset output).
  • CLK/TRG0, CLK/TRG1 and ZC/TO0 go to J6. CLK/TRG2, CLK/TRG3, ZC/TO1 and ZC/TO2 are not connected.
  • The CTC's TO0 is not connected to the bus line OUT0 (SPEC pin 7). OUT0 only passes through this board.

4.5 Interrupt daisy chain

IEO1 (SPEC pin 4, 1 k pull-up R13) → CTC IEI → CTC IEO → PIO IEI → PIO IEO → IEO2 (SPEC pin 5).

  • Priority on this board: CTC highest, then PIO, then whatever takes IEO2 as its IEI on another board.
  • R13 holds the top of the chain high, which makes the CPU board the head of the chain. A board that drove IEO1 would sit above the CTC in priority. To confirm against the I/O and VDP boards.
  • VDPINTB (SPEC pin 3) is not connected to /INT or to a CTC trigger on this board. It only passes through. (On a real MTX, the VDP interrupt goes to CTC channel 0's trigger.) To confirm how VDP interrupts reach the Z80 in the Z80 SBC configuration.

4.6 Clock

  • JJP1 ("PHI SOURCE"): pin 1 = bus 4M (SPEC pin 6), pin 2 = CLK, pin 3 = OSC (XT1 output).
    • 2–3: local oscillator drives CLK. CLK goes out on CTRL pin 2, and the 4M line is left undriven by this board.
    • 1–2: CLK comes from the bus 4M line (e.g. from another board).
  • R18 (330 R) pulls CLK up to +5 V. That's the classic fix for TTL oscillators, which don't reach the Z80's clock VIH (VCC − 0.6 V). With a CMOS oscillator it isn't needed, and it costs about 15 mA every low half-cycle.
  • XT1's enable pin (pin 1) is unconnected. That's fine for most DIP-14 cans, where pin 1 is either n/c or has an internal pull-up.

4.7 Reset

  • MAX811L (IC7): holds /RESET (net RESB) low for at least 140 ms after +5 V rises above about 4.63 V, and whenever VCC dips below that.
  • Its /MR input (internal pull-up) is pulled low by SW1 or by the bus line KBDRST (SPEC pin 8), e.g. from a keyboard board. The MAX811 debounces both.
  • RESB and the programmer's RESX (PROG pin 6, active high, 1 k pull-down) both go into the PLD, which drives RSTB to the CPU /RESET, the CTC /RESET and CTRL pin 1. That is the system reset for the whole stack.
  • PLD logic: RSTB low when RESB is low or RESX is high.

4.8 Power

  • PL1 centre pin = net PWR. Sleeve and switch contacts = GND.
  • PWR goes unswitched to PROG pin 8, so 12 V is on the stack bus whenever the jack is plugged in, even with SW2 off.
  • SW2 pole 1 switches PWR → V+ (DC-DC Vin). SW2 pole 2 switches GND → V− (DC-DC pad 1, marked GND). If the module's two GND pads are connected internally (as on most small buck modules), pole 2 does nothing useful. To confirm.
  • PW1 Vout = +5 V for the whole stack (SPEC pin 2). D2 (via R3 470 R) shows +5 V is present.
  • Input: 12 V DC (the module is a small bought-in 12 V → 5 V buck converter).
  • Copper: +5 V pour on F.Cu, GND pour on B.Cu.

5. Memory and I/O map (from the PLD)

Memory

CPU address Device Select
0000–7FFF 28C256 ROM (32 K) CSB0: /MREQ low, /IORQ high, A15 = 0 — or programmer ROMCSX high
8000–FFFF 62256 RAM (32 K) CSB1: /MREQ low, /IORQ high, A15 = 1

ROM address 0 = CPU 0000, which is the Z80 reset vector. Programs for this board are therefore assembled with ORG 0 and burnt as-is by the programmer.

I/O

Device Ports Registers
CTC 18h–1Bh (A7–A2 = 000110) channel 0, 1, 2, 3
PIO 20h–23h (A7–A2 = 001000) A data, B data, A control, B control

Decode is full on A2–A7, so there are no mirrors within 00–FF. These are not the MTX port addresses (the MTX board has its own map).

The PLD doesn't see A8–A15, so I/O decode is 8-bit. IN r,(C) style accesses with B ≠ 0 still hit the same ports.

Equations (WinCUPL, Device g16v8a; the .jed is simple mode, g16v8as):

!CSB0 = (!A15 & !MREQB & IORQB) # ROMCSX ;          /* ROM  0000-7FFF, or programmer */
!CSB1 =   A15 & !MREQB & IORQB ;                    /* RAM  8000-FFFF */
!CSB2 = !A7 & !A6 &  A5 & !A4 & !A3 & !A2 & !IORQB ; /* PIO  20-23 */
!CSB3 = !A7 & !A6 & !A5 &  A4 &  A3 & !A2 & !IORQB ; /* CTC  18-1B */
!RSTB = RESX # !RESB ;                              /* reset */
  • The fuse map (.jed, built 2023-07-22, name z80-003b-remapped-v001) was decoded term by term and matches these equations. The .PLD file was saved later (2023-12-04), but the logic is the same.
  • The source comments are out of date: they say ROM is in the "top 32k" and RAM in the "lower 32k". The equations, and the board's behaviour, are the reverse: ROM low, RAM high.
  • /RD and /M1 aren't PLD inputs. WRB is wired to pin 11 but unused, so pin 11 is effectively a spare input.

Programming-mode behaviour. The programmer gets the bus with /BUSRQ, not /WAIT (/WAIT isn't on the stack bus; it's only pulled up on this board). In bus-request acknowledge the Z80 floats A0–A15, D0–D7 and /MREQ, /IORQ, /RD, /WR. (Under /WAIT it would keep driving them, which is why BUSRQ is the right mechanism.) So while the programmer holds the bus, the Z80 has floated /MREQ, /IORQ, /RD and /WR, and none of them has a pull-up on this board (section 9, item 2). The PLD doesn't mask the other selects with ROMCSX, but:

  • RAM is safe as long as the image is under 32 K, because the programmer drives A15 = 0.
  • PIO/CTC could see /CE asserted if a floating /IORQ drifts low while the programmer's A7–A2 match 18h–1Bh or 20h–23h. They would only drive the data bus if /RD also floated low. Unlikely, but not prevented.

8. Timing notes

  • Memory decode delay: ATF16V8B-15 adds up to 15 ns between /MREQ or the address and the chip select.
  • I/O decode isn't gated by /M1, but Zilog peripherals treat /M1 + /IORQ as interrupt acknowledge, so that's fine (see below).
  • Rough opcode-fetch budget (the tightest cycle): /MREQ-to-data-needed is about 1.5 T − tMREQ delay − tdata setup. At the fitted 4 MHz (T = 250 ns) that leaves roughly 220–250 ns after the PLD delay. A 150 ns 28C256 and a 70 ns 62256 both have comfortable margin, so memory timing isn't a concern on this build.
  • If a later revision runs faster: at 8 MHz (T = 125 ns) the same budget falls to roughly 90 ns, which a 150 ns EEPROM doesn't meet, and /WAIT isn't available to add a wait state. Figures are approximate; work them through with the actual Z84C00 speed grade.
  • Refresh cycles: the PLD can't see /RFSH, so ROM or RAM is selected during refresh (/MREQ low, refresh address on the bus). With /OE tied low the selected chip drives the data bus briefly. That's harmless because nobody reads it, but it adds switching current.
  • Interrupt acknowledge: the PLD doesn't see /M1, so a PIO/CTC select can coincide with an INTA cycle if the PC's A2–A7 happen to match. Both Zilog parts recognise /M1 + /IORQ as INTA and won't do a register read without /RD, so this is benign.

9. Observations for the next revision

Most important first.

  1. Bus contention on writes. Both memories have /OE permanently active, so the selected chip drives D0–D7 while the CPU is also driving it.
    • RAM: from /CS falling (T1) until /WR falls (T2), on every RAM write. That's roughly one T-state of contention.
    • ROM: for the whole cycle of any write to 0000–7FFF, because CSB0 isn't gated by /WR (WRB reaches the PLD but isn't used). Normal code doesn't write there, but a stray pointer would.
    • Fixes: drive RAM /OE from /RD. For the ROM, re-route pin 11 from /WR to /RD and use !CSB0 = (!A15 & !MREQB & !RDB) # ROMCSX. That needs no extra parts.
  2. No pull-ups on /MREQ, /IORQ, /RD, /WR or the address bus. These float while the programmer holds the bus (BUSRQ). Floating PLD inputs can create spurious selects, and floating CMOS inputs draw extra current. Add about 10 k pull-ups on the control strobes.
  3. Almost no decoupling. Only three 1 µF caps for seven ICs plus an oscillator, and none at the CPU, PIO, CTC or PLD. Add 100 nF at every IC supply pin. The planned ground plane will help, but local bypassing is still needed.
  4. Floating CTC inputs. CLK/TRG2 and CLK/TRG3 are unconnected, and TRG0/TRG1 float when J6 is empty. In counter mode a floating trigger counts noise, and every floating CMOS input draws current. Tie them to GND or +5 V through resistors, or bring all four out to J6.
  5. No buffering on the CPU bus. The Z80 drives every stacked board directly. With a full stack (I/O, VDP, programmer, maybe more) the capacitive load and the 0.15 mm tracks will slow the edges. Consider 74HCT245 / 74HCT541 buffers, or at least series termination on CLK and the strobes.
  6. /WAIT, /NMI, /HALT, /RFSH, /BUSACK aren't on the bus. Slow peripherals can't stretch cycles, nothing can raise an NMI, and the programmer can't confirm bus ownership. If a revision of the bus allows spare pins (PROG pin 1 SP3, AUX pins), routing /BUSACK and /WAIT would be worth it.
  7. 12 V on the bus. PROG pin 8 (PWR) carries the 12 V barrel-jack input, unswitched (live even with SW2 off). It sits next to ROMOEX (pin 7), which goes straight to the 28C256's /OE. A bent pin, a solder bridge or a board stacked one pin out would put 12 V on 5 V logic. On the programmer, PWR also reaches J5 pin 2. Options: take it off the bus, move it away from logic pins, or at least switch and fuse it. Label it clearly either way.
  8. Power switch second pole. If PW1's GND pads are common inside the module, SW2 pole 2 is redundant. No harm, but the switch could become SPST, or pole 2 could switch PWR to the bus as well.
  9. No input protection. There's no reverse-polarity diode, fuse or TVS on the barrel jack.
  10. R18 330 R clock pull-up. Not needed with a CMOS oscillator, and it loads the oscillator (about 15 mA in each low half-cycle at 4 MHz). Keep it only if TTL oscillators will be fitted; otherwise use about 1 k or leave it out.
  11. VDP interrupt routing. VDPINTB and OUT0 are pass-through only. For MTX-style software (VDP interrupt on CTC channel 0's trigger), consider a jumper from VDPINTB to CLK/TRG0.
  12. Programmer reset vs. MAX811. RESX bypasses the MAX811 and goes through the PLD. That's fine, but the reset pulse width then depends on the programmer firmware (200–500 ms). It's comfortably long.
